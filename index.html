Autonomous and Interpretable Decomposition of Degraded RGB Documents

Supervised by Prof. Mohamed Cheriet and Saida Khazri, Synchromédia Lab

Old manuscripts degrade: ink fades or bleeds through the page, paper yellows, stains appear. Most archives are scanned in plain RGB, which keeps only three values per pixel, while the page holds more physical layers than that. Many different mixes of ink, paper and damage explain the same pixel, so separating them is an underdetermined inverse problem.

The question: can an interpretable model that chooses its own number of layers be constrained to recover meaningful components anyway? The plan is to build a benchmark with layer-level ground truth, adapt the lab's PRISM model to RGB with sparsity, spatial and physical constraints, and test it on handwriting recovery.

Multimodal Deep/Machine Learning for Pediatric Appendicitis Diagnosis

Ouadahi Mohand Belkacem, et al. Target journal to be decided.

Ultrasound reading of pediatric appendicitis depends heavily on the operator, and single-modality models have shown limited reliability. This study combines ultrasound images and clinical data from the Regensburg Pediatric Appendicitis Dataset (782 patients, 2,097 images, 58 clinical features).

Read the results

On clinical data, XGBoost reaches 94.23% accuracy and 0.9746 ROC-AUC with only 2 false negatives on the held-out test set. For images, the proposed ScaleAware-Attention architecture beats the published image-only state of the art by 0.06 AUROC. A fusion network joining the vision module with a GANDALF-based tabular branch through cross-attention reaches 0.9609 AUROC, with 100% recall on healthy patients and 100% precision on appendicitis predictions, a +19.7 point AUROC gain over the best image-only model.
